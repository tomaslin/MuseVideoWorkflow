# Film-school language: mise-en-scene, editing, sound

## Mise-en-scene (everything in the frame)

Mise-en-scene is everything the camera sees, placed on purpose: setting,
costume, lighting, actor position and movement, and props. A prop that
appears in shot 3 and pays off in shot 20 is a choice; a prop that appears
once and vanishes is clutter. The look bible records the world rules:
what objects recur, what rooms look like, what the weather and time of day
are allowed to be. If a shot breaks a world rule, the critique must name it.

## Editing grammar

- **Cut:** the basic join. A cut should land on movement, a look, or a
  sound — not in the middle of nothing.
- **Match cut:** two shots joined by a shared shape, movement, or idea.
  The classic continuity device; use it deliberately at sequence joints.
- **Jump cut:** a cut within the same setup that skips time. It creates
  urgency or unease. Accidental jump cuts (same framing, slightly different
  position) read as mistakes, not style.
- **Cross-cutting:** alternating between two places or times to build
  tension or comparison.
- **Montage:** a series of short shots that compress time or build an idea.
  A montage still needs an arc; a montage of pretty shots with no change
  is a trailer, not a sequence.
- **Kuleshov effect:** meaning comes from juxtaposition. A neutral face
  cut to food reads as hunger; the same face cut to a grave reads as grief.
  Shot order is therefore a writing decision, and the EDL is a script.
- **Rhythm:** shot length is pacing. Short shots accelerate; long shots
  hold tension or let a performance breathe. A project that cuts every
  10 seconds by generation necessity must still vary its internal rhythm
  through movement, stillness, and sound.

## Sound design

- **Dialogue:** carries story and character. Recorded as separate stems.
- **Music:** carries emotion and time. Mood-described in this pipeline,
  never a specific copyrighted recording.
- **Sound effects (SFX):** carry place and touch — a door, rain, a kettle,
  footsteps. One or two precise sounds per shot beat a wash of noise.
- **Silence / room tone:** silence is a sound choice. A held silence after
  a revelation is often the most powerful sound in a short video.
- **Mix rule:** spoken words above music, always. Music never obscures a
  line that changes meaning.

## What "beautiful" means here

Beautiful is not "more effects". It is: one locked look, held across every
shot; light with a direction; color with a rule; composition with an idea;
editing with a reason for every cut; and sound that leaves room for
silence. The critique rubric scores those things, not prettiness in the
abstract.
