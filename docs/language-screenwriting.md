# Screenwriting language for shot and sequence definitions

Use these terms with their standard meanings. A definition that uses them
loosely is a definition that will drift in generation.

## Structure

- **Premise:** one sentence. Who wants what, what stands in the way, what
  it costs. If the premise needs a paragraph, it is not a premise yet.
- **Beat:** the smallest unit of change. A beat is not a line of dialogue;
  it is a shift in power, knowledge, or feeling. Every shot in this pipeline
  should contain exactly one beat.
- **Sequence:** a chain of beats with one dramatic question. A sequence
  ends when that question is answered or replaced.
- **Act:** setup, confrontation, resolution — used loosely for short work.
  A 4-minute video usually has three sequences, not three acts in the
  feature sense. Do not force a feature structure onto a short.
- **Reversal:** a beat where the expected outcome flips. A video without a
  reversal is a mood reel, not a story.
- **Cliffhanger:** an unresolved question at a sequence boundary, strong
  enough to carry the viewer into the next sequence. "To be continued" is
  not a cliffhanger; a new fact that changes the last scene is.
- **Subtext:** what a character means but does not say. Dialogue that
  states the subtext aloud is exposition, and it reads as exposition on
  screen. Prefer action that shows it.
- **Exposition:** information the viewer needs. Deliver it through action,
  an object, or a choice — never through a character explaining the plot
  to someone who already knows it.

## Dialogue rules for this pipeline

- Every line must do one of: reveal character, advance the story, or
  change a relationship. Lines that do none of these are cut.
- Preserve negation, numbers, dates, conditions, and uncertainty exactly.
  A changed "might" to "did" is a changed story, not a paraphrase.
- Dialogue lives in the audio script, not in the video prompt. Video
  prompts describe what is seen and the sound mood; they never carry a
  promise that a character will say a specific line on camera in sync.

## What a script is not

A script is not an outline, a teaser, or a list of vibes. The pipeline's
script stage requires a complete, performable script: every beat written,
every sequence closed or deliberately cliffhung, no `[insert scene]`,
no `[TBD]`. The validator rejects placeholder text.
