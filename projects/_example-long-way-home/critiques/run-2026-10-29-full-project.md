# Critical analysis 2: The full 24-shot plan, 2026-10-29

Automated evaluation (re-run after tension values were set):
overall 9.4/10, shipping-ready by the tool's own rule, with one
finding — s16 flagged as a possible two-action shot. That score is
real but narrow: it measures coverage, locked blocks, variety, sound
fields, tension shape, and continuity of text. It does not measure
whether the story works. This analysis does that, benchmark by
benchmark, against `docs/reference-benchmarks.md`.

## Opening (s01–s08): 7/10

See the opening-run analysis for s01–s03. The rest of the sequence
(s04 hallway listening, s05 kitchen countertop, s06–s08 workshop
discovery) is stronger: the house itself becomes a character, and
the workshop tools "left in place" is the first real story detail.
But the opening's missing detail (analysis 1, point 2) means the
workshop lands as a new idea at s09 rather than a payoff of a planted
one. The fix in analysis 1 would lift this whole sequence.

## Midpoint reversal (s09–s16): 6/10 — the weakest part of the film

The plan's midpoint is the letter: read, then folded, not torn.
As an idea it is excellent — refusal as reversal, internal rather
than explosive. As a shot plan it is under-built. The letter is
introduced and resolved within a few shots, with no earlier plant
(the letter is not in the opening, not in the kitchen, not in the
workshop discovery) and no close-up that lets the audience read the
man's face while he decides. A reversal that the audience cannot see
happen is a narration, not a scene. And s16, the evaluator's flagged
shot, sits right in this stretch — a two-action shot at the exact
moment the film most needs a clean, single beat.

Fixes, specific:
- Plant the letter (or its envelope) in s05–s08, in the kitchen or
  workshop, before it is read.
- Give the decision a close-up: one shot, one action — "folds the
  letter" — with no other verb competing for the 10 seconds.
- Split s16 into two shots, or cut its second action.

## Climax (s15 in tension terms, s17–s24 in sequence terms): 8/10

Tension now peaks at 9 at shot 15 and resolves to 2 by shot 24, which
is the correct shape and was not true before tension values were
set — before this run, the curve was literally unverified (all
tension values null). That is a system criticism, not just a film
one: the example shipped for hours with a tension arc claimed in
prose and absent in data. The evaluator now catches that; it should
have caught it on day one. (Fixed in this run: tension values are
now recorded per panel.)

The climax itself — finishing the chair — is visual and earned, but
it is quiet for a 9. A quiet climax can work, but only if the sound
design carries it: the plan's sound cues here are thin (sanding,
dust in light) and there is no planned silence after the final
stroke. Add one deliberate silence beat at the finish; otherwise
the 9 will read as a 7.

## Resolution (s19–s24): 9/10

The strongest sequence. Gate out in morning light rhymes with gate
in at dusk, house lit instead of dark, chair finished. The rhyme is
exact without being a repeat, and the tension fall (9 → 2) is
recorded, not just intended. The only criticism: the porch light
being left on is the film's last image and its most important
symbol, and it currently has no dedicated close-up. Give it one.

## Sound across the film: 6/10

Every shot has a sound cue field, which is why the evaluator scores
sound 10/10 — but a field is not a mix. The plan has no loudness
target, no dialogue/music hierarchy (there is no dialogue, which
helps), no planned silence until this analysis proposes one, and no
SFX list that a mixer could actually build from. The sound-cue
schema exists; the mix plan does not yet use it. See chapter 09 —
it describes a mix plan, but the example project does not contain
one. That gap between schema and example is the system's recurring
weakness: documentation ahead of worked example.

## Continuity: 8/10

Text continuity is perfect (locked blocks verbatim, no drift
detected). Visual continuity is unanchored (no reference images
generated yet for the man, the house, or the chair). The score
flatters the plan. Until reference images exist, continuity should
be reported as "text-locked, visually unanchored" in every report.

## Overall: a good plan with a soft middle, not a finished film

The plan scores 9.4/10 on verifiable fields and roughly 7/10 on
story benchmarks. The gap between those two numbers is the most
important finding of this run: the automated evaluator is necessary
and not sufficient, and any report that quotes only the automated
score is misleading. Both numbers, and the human watch, belong in
every shipping decision.
