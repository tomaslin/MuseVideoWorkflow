# Lore bible: The Long Way Home

This is a realistic drama, not a fantasy, so "lore" means the
world's rules about the house, the objects, and the man — the
rules that, if broken, break the story's logic, not just its
continuity.

## The house

- The house is empty but kept: dust, not ruin. Windows are
  intact, the roof does not leak, the porch is swept at the end.
  If the house were ruined, the story would be about loss of a
  place; it is about a place kept and a life resumed.
- The house is the house the man built. He knows where every
  creak is, which countertop is worn, which window sticks.
  He does not explore it like a stranger; he returns to it
  like a man returning to a body he knows.
- Time of day is a rule, not a backdrop: arrival at golden
  hour, reckoning in late light and rain, release in morning
  light and dusk with the porch light on. Light must progress
  in that order. A morning-light shot in the arrival sequence
  would break the film's clock.

## The objects — each one has a rule

- **The chair:** half-finished at the start, finished at the
  end. It is never a different chair, never a bought chair,
  never finished early. Its progress is the film's progress.
- **The letter:** unsent, read once, folded carefully, never
  torn, never mailed, never read aloud. The choice not to
  destroy it is the midpoint reversal. Tearing it would be a
  different film.
- **The photograph:** faded, on the hallway wall, looked at
  once in s06, then looked away from, not smiling. It is never
  taken down, never shown in close-up as a readable image,
  never explained. Explanation would turn grief into exposition.
- **The telephone:** silent, on the wall, looked at in s13 and
  turned away from. No call is made, no call arrives. The rule
  is: no call will fix this.
- **The porch light:** switched on at dusk in s22, left on
  in s23–s24. The house is left lit, not locked in darkness.
  Turning it off at the end would reverse the film's meaning.

## The man

- He moves slowly and deliberately throughout. He never hurries,
  never runs, never dances. Speed would break the character.
- He speaks little and, in this film's clips, not at all: the
  film's sound is room tone, weather, and work, not dialogue.
  Adding dialogue in a clip would change the film's language.
- His wardrobe never changes: worn brown work jacket, faded
  denim shirt, dark trousers, scuffed boots, in every sequence.
  The jacket's wear is the same in s01 and s24; only the light
  on it changes.

## Violations — a checklist for reviewers

- House ruined, or a new room that contradicts the house's layout.
- Chair finished before s17, or a different chair substituted.
- Letter torn, mailed, or read aloud.
- Photograph explained, taken down, or shown as a readable image.
- Telephone call made or received.
- Porch light off at the end, or house left dark.
- Man hurrying, running, or speaking in a clip.
- Morning light in the arrival sequence, or golden hour in
  the release sequence's morning shots.
