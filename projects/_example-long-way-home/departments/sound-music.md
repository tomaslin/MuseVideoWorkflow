# Sound and music: The Long Way Home

## Sound design per sequence (not a mood — a list)

- **Arrival (s01–s08):** wind in dry grass, distant birds,
  gate latch, gravel, wood creak, room tone, a clock ticking
  far off, silence held in s06. No music. Music in the arrival
  would tell the audience how to feel about a return they
  have not yet understood.
- **Reckoning (s09–s16):** door creak, dust in light, wood
  grain and breath, plane on wood rhythmic, paper and silence,
  silence held in s13 and s15, rain on glass in s14, clamp
  click and room tone in s16. No music. The work is the score.
- **Release (s17–s24):** sandpaper, birds, silence then a soft
  exhale in s18, gate latch and birdsong in s19, footsteps
  and a distant voice in s20, broom on wood in s21, switch
  click and warm lamp hum in s22, evening birds and lamp
  warmth in s23, wind and distant birds with silence under
  it in s24. No music at any point in this film. That is a
  decision, not an omission: a score would narrate what the
  man's face and hands already say.

## Silence as a sound

Silence is used deliberately in s06, s13, s15, and s18, after
a revelation or a decision. Silence is not the absence of a
sound cue; it is the cue. A mix that fills those silences with
music or room tone at full level would erase the film's
quietest and most important beats.

## Mix targets (for the assembly stage)

- Room tone and weather audible but below work sounds.
- Work sounds (plane, sandpaper, clamp, broom) above room tone.
- Silence beats: room tone pulled down, not up, for the held
  seconds. Dialogue: none in clips, by design.
