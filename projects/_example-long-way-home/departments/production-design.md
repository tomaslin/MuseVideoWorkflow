# Production design: The Long Way Home

## Locations (one house, three states of light)

- **The drive:** dirt, dry grass at the edges, a garden gate
  at the house end, no pavement, no modern fencing. The drive
  in s01 (golden hour, house dark) and s24 (dusk, house lit)
  must be the same drive, shot from the same side, so the
  rhyme reads.
- **The porch:** wooden, with a broom (s21), a light switch
  (s22), and a front door that is never shown from inside
  opening outward in a way that contradicts s03's pause.
- **The hallway:** faded wallpaper, a photograph on the wall
  (s06), a mirror (s15), a telephone on the wall (s13). The
  hallway's layout is fixed: front door at one end, kitchen
  at the other, workshop door off the side. No shot may invent
  a hallway that contradicts that.
- **The kitchen:** worn countertop (s05), a chair by the
  window (s08), late light through the window. The countertop
  wear is a continuity anchor: the same worn patch, same side.
- **The workshop:** beside the house, tools left in place
  (s09), a bench (s12, s16), a window (s14), a wood plane
  and shavings (s11). Tools do not move between shots except
  when the man moves them to work.

## Background layers

Every shot's background names foreground, midground, and deep
background, and what moves in each. In this film, the background
is usually still — the house, the room, the light — and the
movement is the man, the light, or the weather. A background
that moves for no reason (a passing car, a new object) would
break the film's stillness, which is its language.
