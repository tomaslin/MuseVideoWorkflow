# Desert Beggars vs the Jiangshi — treatment

## The band

The Beggar Sect of the Western Reaches is not an army. It is a dozen
men and women in patched robes, led by Chief Hong, a one-eyed veteran
who carries a knotted dog-beating staff and a wine gourd, and
seconded by Little Sparrow, a young fighter fast enough to run
across the dunes without sinking. They survive on what caravans
throw away. That is the point: they have nothing to lose, and
nothing to protect except each other and the people who scorn them.

## The threat

A sandstorm uncovers a buried tomb-city in the desert realms. At
dusk, the dead rise as jiangshi — hopping vampires in rotting Qing
robes, arms stiff before them, paper talismans fluttering on their
foreheads. They do not run. They do not tire. They hop, in formation,
toward the caravanserai's lights.

## The trailer's three movements

1. **Desert awakening (s01–s03):** Dawn over the dunes. The band
   crosses the desert, ragged and laughing. Chief Hong plants his
   staff at a ruined caravanserai and looks toward the storm on the
   horizon. The joke lands first; the dread lands second.
2. **The threat (s04–s06):** Night. The jiangshi hop out of the
   sand tombs in a line. Chief Hong meets the first one staff to
   stiff arm. A talisman flutters loose — and the vampire's eyes
   open fully. The trailer's scare beat.
3. **Battle and hook (s07–s09):** The full band fights, wire-fu
   across the dunes: staffs, bowls, ropes, a leap that clears three
   vampires at once. Dawn breaks. The band stands silhouetted
   against the rising sun over a field of settling dust — and in
   the far distance, a second tomb door grinds open. Cut to title.
   The question is not "did they win?" It is "what woke them?"
