# Lore bible: Desert Beggars vs the Jiangshi

Rules are stated as rules. A rule that is not written will be
broken by the next prompt. Every shot's `loreNotes` must name
the rule it exercises, and any shot that breaks a rule is a
failed shot, however pretty.

## The jianghu and the beggar sect

- The jianghu is the martial world of wandering sects, not a
  kingdom and not an army. Sects guard roads, towns, and
  reputations. The beggar sect is the lowest-ranked sect, and
  the only one that does not guard a road — it survives on
  what others discard.
- Beggar sect hierarchy: Chief Hong leads by seniority and by
  the staff, not by birth. The staff is passed, not inherited.
  Sparrow is second not by rank but by speed — she is the
  sect's scout and messenger, which is why she is on the roof
  in s03.
- Beggar fighting style: practical, ragged, and improvised.
  Staffs, bowls, ropes, and anything to hand. No swords, no
  ornate weapons — a beggar with a sword has stolen it, and
  the story would need to say so. Hong's staff technique is
  the Dog-Beating Staff style: low arcs, trips, and blocks,
  never high flourishes.

## The desert realms

- Three locations only: open dunes, ruined caravanserai,
  sand tombs. The desert realms are not a map to be explored;
  they are a corridor between the caravanserai and the tombs.
  Adding a fourth location in a trailer would dilute the
  trailer's geography.
- The caravanserai is ruined but not abandoned: lanterns are
  lit at night, water jars are full, the well works. People
  pass through; they do not live there. That is why the
  beggars are the only defenders when the tombs open.
- The sandstorm in s03 is not weather for atmosphere. It is
  the mechanism that uncovers the tombs. Cause and effect must
  be clear: storm → tombs uncovered → dead rise at dusk.

## Jiangshi — the rules, without exception

1. Jiangshi hop. They never walk, never run, never crawl.
   Rigor mortis stiffens them; hopping is the only locomotion
   their bodies allow.
2. Jiangshi are blind. They track breath and movement, not
   sight. They turn toward sound and toward the living, not
   toward a face.
3. Jiangshi do not speak, do not breathe visibly, and do not
   tire. They hop at a steady rhythm until stopped.
4. A paper talisman pasted on the forehead seals a jiangshi.
   While sealed, its eyes are closed or downcast and its
   hopping is slower. When the talisman loosens or falls, its
   eyes open fully and it becomes faster and more dangerous.
   This is the s05–s06 mechanism, and it must happen to exactly
   one vampire, the lead, in this trailer.
5. Jiangshi hold their arms stiffly forward, fingers together.
   Arms never relax, never swing, never gesture.
6. Jiangshi wear the robes they were buried in: dark Qing
   robes with faded gold trim. They do not change clothes,
   do not carry weapons, and do not use tools.
7. Jiangshi are stopped, not killed, by staff blows in this
   trailer. No vampire is destroyed on screen in s05–s08;
   they are knocked back, staggered, and dust-burst. The
   trailer's stakes depend on the vampires being unstoppable
   by ordinary blows — if Hong destroys one with a single
   staff strike, the threat collapses.

## Violations — a checklist for reviewers

- A jiangshi running, speaking, or holding a weapon: violation.
- A talisman falling from any vampire other than the lead in
  s06: violation.
- A beggar with a sword or ornate weapon: violation, unless
  the script explains it.
- A fourth location, a modern object, or neon light: violation.
- A vampire destroyed in a single blow: violation of rule 7.
- Eyes open before s06: violation of rule 4.
